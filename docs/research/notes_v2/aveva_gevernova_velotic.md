## Q1. What does each product do, in plain words, and which KPIs and analytics/OR methods does it use?

### Takeaway
All three vendors build the same layered stack: (1) connect to machines, (2) store time-stamped data in a historian, (3) run production on top of it with MES (orders, OEE, downtime, quality, genealogy), (4) show it on dashboards/HMI, and (5) add analytics (root cause, SPC, predictive models, digital-twin "what-if" simulation, finite-capacity scheduling). OptiProductio is a small copy of layers 1 to 4 plus a bit of layer 5. The KPIs the vendors repeat most are OEE (availability × performance × quality), downtime by reason, throughput, scrap/first-pass quality, MTBF/MTTR, schedule adherence and WIP.

### Inferences
- The same OR and industrial-engineering ideas OptiProductio uses show up in the vendors' products: OEE (Proficy MES, RTPPM, AVEVA MES, Insight), downtime reason codes and Pareto-style loss ranking (RTPPM reason trees, DPM loss prioritisation, AVEVA delay accounting), WIP visibility (Tracker), bottleneck/constraint analysis (DPM, Scheduler), finite-capacity scheduling with what-if (Scheduler), SPC (both MESs), and predictive anomaly detection (CSense, AVEVA Predictive Analytics). A CONWIP cap alert is not a named feature anywhere, so it is a good original angle for the student. It is closest to Tracker's WIP visibility plus an alert rule.
- AVEVA's "delay accounting against MSR" is a useful framing for the student: measure the gap between actual output and the best sustainable rate, then classify every lost minute. This is OEE thinking expressed in time.
- The vendors split "historian" from "MES" for good reasons: raw time-stamped events are stored once and never edited, and the KPIs are computed on top. OptiProductio can copy this with a raw event log (CSV/SQLite) plus a KPI layer.

## Q2. What does the GE Vernova digital-twin blog say about simulation improving operations?

### Takeaway
The blog is a short vendor interview (Steve Pavlosky, VP Product Management, Proficy). It says process digital twins built in Proficy CSense let manufacturers test changes in simulation before applying them, predict asset and process performance, and auto-adjust setpoints. It gives three anonymised customer results (−33% weight complaints, +10% throughput, and a qualitative pulp-and-paper case) with no methodology.

### Cited Findings
- Author: Steve Pavlosky, Vice President, Product Management, GE Vernova Proficy Software & Services. "Last updated" Oct 15, 2025; page metadata suggests it was first published in 2024. Format: about a 3-minute interview-style post. — [GE Vernova blog](https://www.gevernova.com/software/blog/how-digital-twin-simulation-software-improves-manufacturing-operations)
- Claim: digital-twin process simulation is "key to unlocking enterprise-wide value". — same source
- Claimed uses: monitor production lines, evaluate equipment performance, quantify scrap rates, optimise energy use, solve quality-control problems, recommend asset/process adjustments, **automatically adjust setpoints**, predict future asset and process performance, and **run simulations before deploying changes** to live operations (Proficy CSense). — same source
- Results (vendor claims, anonymised): an international food manufacturer "decreased customer complaints related to product weight by 33%", with better quality and less waste; a pulp and paper maker predicted Critical-to-Quality (CTQ) KPIs to improve productivity and eliminate wastewater regulatory issues (no number); and an advanced process control solution to "increase production system throughput by 10% using optimization technology". — same source
- Non-simulation numbers in the same post: an aviation customer with 32 plants on Proficy Historian for Cloud "reduced infrastructure costs by more than 20%" and eliminated "more than a month of planned downtime". — same source
- The author's advice: start with "low-hanging fruit" and expand after early wins; take a phased approach; secure executive buy-in and an operational-excellence culture; choose tools process engineers can use without a data-science team; and move away from paper and Excel data collection, which causes errors and weak traceability. — same source
- The blog does **not** discuss bottleneck analysis, discrete-event simulation, scheduling, or a formal digital-twin definition. — same source

### Inferences
- In this blog, "simulation" means a data-driven process model (CSense) used for what-if setpoint testing and prediction. It does not mean discrete-event simulation of a production line. OptiProductio would get closer to the classic IE/OR meaning by adding a small discrete-event or queueing what-if, for example "what happens to throughput and WIP if the WIP cap is 3 vs 5, or if Station 2 cycle time drops 10%?". It could use SimPy or Little's Law (WIP = throughput × flow time).
- The "move off paper/Excel" advice is the same argument OptiProductio makes for IR-sensor counting over manual tally sheets.

### Gaps
- The blog gives no baselines, sample sizes, timeframes or customer names for its 33% and 10% figures, so they cannot be verified.

## Q3. Concrete ideas a low-cost student build can borrow

### Takeaway
Borrow the vendor *patterns*, not their scale: a raw event log ("mini historian"), an OEE card broken into A, P and Q, a downtime reason Pareto, shift and time-window comparisons, a bottleneck indicator, a WIP/CONWIP alert, a simple predictive or what-if panel, and a "case study" stat strip showing before vs after. Each pattern below is traced to the vendor feature it imitates.

### Cited Findings (vendor feature → student version)
- **Store-and-forward historian** (Proficy Historian; PI System buffers data during network drops) → have the Arduino or the Python serial reader append timestamped events to CSV/SQLite and buffer them if the dashboard is closed. — [Historian](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/historian); [AVEVA PI System](https://aveva.com/en/products/aveva-pi-system/)
- **OEE broken into availability × performance × quality** (Smart Factory MES; RTPPM) → three gauges plus the product. Breakdown button time drives A, ideal vs actual cycle time drives P, and the reject button drives Q. — [Smart Factory MES](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/smart-factory-mes); [RTPPM](https://www.velotic.com/products/thingworx/real-time-production-performance-monitoring)
- **Reason trees / downtime reason codes** (RTPPM; AVEVA Production Management delay accounting) → when the breakdown button is pressed, ask for a reason in Streamlit (jam, material shortage, setup) and show a downtime **Pareto chart**. — [RTPPM](https://www.velotic.com/products/thingworx/real-time-production-performance-monitoring); [Production Management](https://www.aveva.com/en/products/production-management/)
- **MTBF / MTTR** (RTPPM) → compute these from breakdown start and end events. — [RTPPM](https://www.velotic.com/products/thingworx/real-time-production-performance-monitoring)
- **Bottleneck and loss prioritisation** (ThingWorx DPM) → flag the station with the highest average cycle time or utilisation as "constraint" and rank losses by minutes of throughput lost. — [DPM](https://www.velotic.com/products/thingworx/digital-performance-management)
- **Closed-loop CI action tracking** (DPM) → a small "actions" table with columns for problem, action, date, and KPI before/after. — [DPM](https://www.velotic.com/products/thingworx/digital-performance-management)
- **Real-time WIP visibility** (Proficy Tracker) → count per buffer (IR in minus IR out) as a bar per station, with the CONWIP cap drawn as a red line and an alert when it is crossed. — [Tracker](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/tracker)
- **SPC rule violations** (AVEVA MES; Proficy MES) → an X-bar or individuals control chart on cycle time with ±3σ limits, highlighting out-of-control points. — [AVEVA MES](https://www.aveva.com/en/products/manufacturing-execution-system/); [Smart Factory MES](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/smart-factory-mes)
- **Shift and time-window comparisons** (whitepaper example queries) → a selector for "this hour vs same hour yesterday" and "shift A vs shift B". — Velotic whitepaper, pp.8–9
- **Hourly throughput, scrap by product, downtime split by type** (whitepaper dashboard spec) → use this as the default dashboard layout. — Velotic whitepaper, p.9
- **Predictive / anomaly detection** (AVEVA Predictive Analytics learns normal behaviour and flags deviation; CSense Predict) → train a simple model, such as logistic regression or a random forest, on the **Bosch Kaggle** data to predict reject probability, or use a z-score anomaly flag on live cycle times. Show *why* each flag fired (top features or rule text), in line with the whitepaper's explainability point. — [Predictive Analytics](https://www.aveva.com/en/products/predictive-analytics/); [CSense](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/csense); Velotic whitepaper, pp.13, 16
- **What-if simulation before changing the line** (CSense Simulate; GE Vernova blog; AVEVA digital twin) → a sliders panel (WIP cap, station cycle times, breakdown rate) running a small SimPy or Little's-Law model that predicts throughput and lead time. Label it a "digital twin (simulation)", and note AVEVA's distinction: it becomes a *twin* only when fed live data. — [CSense](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/csense); [GE Vernova blog](https://www.gevernova.com/software/blog/how-digital-twin-simulation-software-improves-manufacturing-operations); [AVEVA Digital Twin](https://www.aveva.com/en/solutions/digital-transformation/digital-twin/)
- **Alerts** (AVEVA Insight: customisable conditions with mobile/desktop notifications) → Streamlit `st.toast` or a coloured banner for WIP over the cap, OEE below target, or a station idle longer than N seconds. — [AVEVA Insight](https://www.aveva.com/en/products/insight/)
- **Role-based views** (Operations Hub; RTPPM by asset, line, shift and crew) → two tabs: "Operator" (big live numbers) and "Engineer" (trends, Pareto, SPC). — [Operations Hub](https://www.velotic.com/products/proficy/operations-hub)
- **Start small, phase it** (GE Vernova blog: "low-hanging fruit"; whitepaper: targeted use cases, early wins) → justifies a 3-station scope and a staged build in the EL report. — [GE Vernova blog](https://www.gevernova.com/software/blog/how-digital-twin-simulation-software-improves-manufacturing-operations); Velotic whitepaper, pp.15, 17
