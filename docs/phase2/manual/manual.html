Arduino-Based Real-Time WIP Monitoring and Bottleneck Detection

Assembly and setup manual: build the table, wire it, flash it, calibrate it, run the demo

What you are building

A tabletop production line. Boxes slide down a chute, a servo gate lets them in one at a time, and three people work at stations S1, S2 and S3. Six IR sensors watch every box enter and leave every station. The Arduino UNO keeps the WIP count, closes the gate when WIP hits the limit, finds the bottleneck and lights its red LED. A NodeMCU sends everything over Wi-Fi to the laptop, which stores it and shows a live dashboard.

How to use this manual

Go page by page. Every block ends with a CHECK. Do not start the next block until the check passes: a fault found early takes 2 minutes to fix, a fault found at the end takes 2 hours.

Grey ELI5 boxes explain the idea in plain words. The glossary at the bottom of each page explains the terms used on that page.

GLOSSARY FOR THIS PAGE

S1/S2/S3: Station 1, Station 2, Station 3.
Bottleneck: The station that limits how fast the whole line can produce.
IIoT: Industrial Internet of Things: factory machines sending data over a network.

1. Safety and golden rules · 2. Parts check

Golden rules

  1. Unplug everything (USB and the servo adapter) before moving any wire.
  2. All GNDs join together: UNO GND, NodeMCU GND, servo adapter minus, every sensor GND.
  3. Servo power never comes from the UNO 5V pin. It comes from the 5 V 2 A adapter.
  4. Never put 5 V into a NodeMCU pin. UNO to NodeMCU goes through the 1 kΩ / 2.2 kΩ divider.
  5. Capacitor polarity: long leg +, striped leg -. Backwards, it can pop.
  6. LED polarity: long leg + (towards the resistor and the UNO pin), short leg to GND.
  7. Hot glue gun and cutter: one person uses it at a time, on a cutting mat, gun on its stand.
  8. Test each block before the next (the CHECK boxes).
ELI5 GND is the 'sea level' that every board measures height from. If two boards have different sea levels, a 5 V signal from one can look like 2 V or 8 V to the other. Joining all GNDs gives everyone the same sea level.

About the 5 V 2 A adapter

If it ends in a round barrel plug, either buy a DC female barrel jack with screw terminals (about ₹20 at any SP Road shop) or cut the plug off and strip the two wires. Find + and - with the multimeter before connecting (DC volts: red probe on the wire that reads +5 V). Mark + with red tape.

4. Build 1: base board and layout

  1. Tape 3 foam boards edge to edge (both sides, packing tape) into a 120 x 45 cm base. Put it on a flat table.
  2. Draw two lines along the length: back strip 9 cm from the back edge, front strip 11 cm from the front edge. The 25 cm between them is the working area.
  3. Mark the chute at the left end (0-30 cm), then the gate at 32 cm.
  4. Mark the 13 x 13 cm platforms: S1 at 36-49 cm, S2 at 61-74 cm, S3 at 86-99 cm, done bin 104-118 cm. The 12 cm gaps are the transfer zones.
  5. Glue foam strips (1.5 cm high) as guide rails so the lane is 12 cm wide (boxes are about 6 cm). Boxes always slide along the lane from left to right.
  6. Mark two dashed QUEUE squares on the front side of the lane, before S2 and before S3. Waiting boxes sit there, then get slid into the lane.
ELI5 The lane is a railway track. Boxes may only travel along it, left to right, because the sensors are like ticket gates at each station's entry and exit. A box lifted over a gate does not get counted.

Why these spacings

  • 13 cm platforms fit one 6 cm box with room for hands.
  • Each platform has an IN sensor at its left edge and an OUT sensor at its right edge, so a box on the platform is between the two sensors.
  • 12 cm gaps keep one station's OUT sensor from seeing the next station's box.
  • The 3D model shows a battery pack for the servo: we use the 5 V 2 A adapter instead.
CHECK before moving on Base is flat and stiff; platform and sensor positions are marked; a box slides freely along the full lane.
GLOSSARY FOR THIS PAGE
S1/S2/S3: Station 1, Station 2, Station 3.
WIP: Work in progress: boxes that have entered the line (passed S1 IN) but not yet left it (passed S3 OUT).
Cycle time: Time a station spends on one box, from its IN sensor to its OUT sensor.

5. Build 2: chute and servo gate

Chute

  1. Cut a foam strip 30 x 10 cm (floor) and two side walls 30 x 4 cm. Glue the walls on, inner width about 7.5 cm (box + 1.5 cm).
  2. Cover the floor with packing tape, shiny side up. Cardboard on bare foam board sticks; on tape it slides.
  3. Make a riser for the high (left) end. Start at 8 cm. A 3-4 cm rise (about 7°) is usually too shallow for cardboard.
  4. Test with one box at a time: put it at the top and let go. It must slide all the way down 10 out of 10 times. If it stops, raise the riser 1 cm and retest. If it hits too hard and bounces, lower it or add a foam bumper.
  5. Glue the low end so the box leaves the chute level with the lane, straight into S1.

Servo gate

  1. Glue a foam block (about 5 x 4 x 6 cm) beside the chute exit. Hot-glue the SG90 on top with its shaft pointing across the chute.
  2. Do not fix the arm yet. First the servo must be at its 'closed' angle: that is done in Calibration (section 14) with the test sketch.
  3. Then push the single-arm horn on, and hot-glue a popsicle stick to the horn so it hangs down across the chute exit (closed). When the servo turns, the stick swings up and one box slides out.
  4. Glue a small foam stop on the far side for the arm to rest against, so 8 boxes pushing on it cannot force it open.
ELI5 The gate is a nightclub bouncer with a clicker. While the club (the line) has fewer people (WIP) than the limit, the bouncer lets one person in. When it is full, nobody new gets in until someone leaves through the exit (S3 OUT).
CHECK before moving on Chute: 10/10 boxes slide all the way. Gate: servo glued firmly, arm NOT yet attached (it is set in Calibration).
GLOSSARY FOR THIS PAGE
CONWIP: Constant WIP: a control rule that lets a new box in only while WIP is below a fixed limit.
Escapement: A gate that lets exactly one item through each time it opens.
WIP: Work in progress: boxes that have entered the line (passed S1 IN) but not yet left it (passed S3 OUT).

6. Build 3: stations, sensor mounts, LED panel, done bin

Platforms

Glue a coloured 13 x 13 cm foam square at S1, S2, S3 (or just outline them with tape). Write S1, S2, S3 big on them.

Sensor mounts (6)

  1. For each sensor cut a small foam block (3 x 3 x 4 cm). Glue it on the back side of the lane, just behind the back rail.
  2. Positions: S1 IN at 36 cm, S1 OUT at 50 cm, S2 IN at 61 cm, S2 OUT at 75 cm, S3 IN at 86 cm, S3 OUT at 100 cm (the dashed red lines in the layout).
  3. Fix each FC-51 to its block with double-sided tape or a dab of glue on the PCB edge only, the two LEDs (IR emitter and receiver) pointing straight across the lane at box mid-height (about 3 cm up).
  4. Cut a notch in the back rail in front of each sensor so the beam is not blocked. Keep the trim pot reachable with a screwdriver.
  5. Write the name on each sensor (S1 IN ...) with marker on tape.
Careful Glue never touches the black and clear LEDs or the trim pot. Keep sensors out of direct sunlight; sunlight contains IR and blinds them. A short tube of black paper around the pair helps near windows.

LED panel

  1. Cut a foam strip 100 x 8 cm and stand it up along the back edge (glue + a foam triangle behind).
  2. For each station, push a green and a red LED through the panel above that station (green left, red right). Write S1, S2, S3 under them.
  3. Bend the legs behind the panel: long leg (+) on top, short leg (-) below, so you can tell them apart when wiring.
  4. Fix the buzzer on the front strip near the UNO, so the team hears it.

Done bin

A small open box (14 x 13 cm) at the right end. Finished boxes are carried back to the chute by hand.

ELI5 Each station has two ticket gates: IN and OUT. The time between a box passing IN and passing OUT is how long the station worked on it (cycle time). With only one gate per station we could not tell working from waiting.
CHECK before moving on 6 sensors mounted at the marked spots, all pointing across the lane, all labelled. LEDs pushed through the panel with + legs on top.

7. Wire 1: breadboard, power and the UNO

Place the UNO and the 400-point breadboard in the middle of the front strip (fix with double-sided tape). 20 cm jumpers are too short for the ends of the table: join one male-female and one male-male jumper (about 40 cm) where needed.

The 400-point breadboard

Each long edge has two rails: red (+) and blue (-). In the middle, each short row of 5 holes is joined; the two halves are separate.

  1. UNO 5V → breadboard red rail (top edge).
  2. UNO GND → breadboard blue rail (top edge).
  3. Join the top and bottom blue rails with a short jumper, and the top and bottom red rails with another, so both edges have power.
  4. This red rail powers only the sensors and nothing else. The servo gets its own supply later.
CHECK before moving on Plug the UNO USB into the laptop. Multimeter on DC volts between red and blue rails: 4.7 to 5.1 V. The UNO's ON LED is lit. Unplug again.

8. Wire 2: the six IR sensors

Each FC-51 has 3 pins: VCC, GND, OUT (read the print on the board; some boards order them differently).

  1. Use male-female jumpers (female end on the sensor). Use the same colours every time: red = VCC, black = GND, a signal colour for OUT.
  2. VCC → breadboard red rail. GND → breadboard blue rail.
  3. OUT → the UNO pin from the table. Do one sensor, then test it (step 5), then the next.
  4. Route the wires along the back strip and under the lane in a neat bundle; tape them down every 15 cm so a hand cannot pull them out.
  5. Test each sensor as you go: plug in the USB, flash the hardware test sketch (Software 1, section 13) and type s. Wave a box in front of the new sensor: its column goes 0 → 1. If it is always 1, turn its trim pot anticlockwise; if never 1, clockwise.

ELI5 The FC-51 is a tiny torch plus a tiny eye. The torch shines invisible infrared light; when a box is close, light bounces back into the eye and the OUT pin drops to LOW (0 V). The little green LED on the module lights when it sees something, which makes tuning easy.

How the UNO counts

The program only counts the moment a sensor changes from 'nothing' to 'box' (an edge), and only if the signal stays steady for 25 ms. After a count, that sensor ignores itself for 0.4 s. So one box = one count, even if the sensor flickers at the box's edge.

Careful Plug the sensor in with power off. VCC and GND swapped will make the module hot within seconds: unplug at once.
CHECK before moving on With the test sketch: each of the 6 columns shows 1 only when a box passes its own sensor, and 0 the rest of the time.

9. Wire 3: LEDs and buzzer

Each LED needs a resistor in series, or it burns out (and can damage the UNO pin).

  1. Put each resistor in the breadboard's middle area, across two different 5-hole rows.
  2. UNO pin → one end of the resistor (jumper into the same 5-hole row).
  3. Other end of the resistor → LED long leg (+), using a male-female jumper (or solder-free: twist and tape).
  4. LED short leg (-) → breadboard blue (GND) rail.
  5. Buzzer: + (longer leg or marked +) → UNO A1. - → blue rail.

10. Wire 4: the servo and its own 5 V supply

The SG90 has 3 wires: orange = signal, red = +5 V, brown = GND.

  1. Use the other (bottom) pair of breadboard rails as a separate servo power rail. First remove the jumper you added in Wire 1 between the top and bottom red rails. Keep the blue rails joined (common GND).
  2. Adapter + → bottom red rail. Adapter - → bottom blue rail (which is joined to the UNO GND).
  3. Push the 470 µF capacitor into the bottom rails: long leg into red (+), striped leg into blue (-).
  4. Servo red → bottom red rail. Servo brown → blue rail. Servo orange → UNO D8.
  5. Double-check: the UNO 5V pin and the adapter + are not connected anywhere.
Careful If the top and bottom red rails are still joined, the adapter's 5 V and the laptop USB's 5 V get tied together. Remove that jumper.

Why a separate supply

When the SG90 starts moving it briefly draws several hundred mA. From the UNO's 5V pin that spike makes the voltage dip and the UNO resets in the middle of the demo (a brown-out). The 2 A adapter has plenty of current, and the capacitor covers the first instant of each spike.

ELI5 The capacitor is a small water tank right next to the tap. When the servo suddenly drinks a lot, it drinks from the tank first, so the pressure for everyone else does not drop.

The program also only sends position pulses for 0.45 s after each move, then lets the servo rest. That stops buzzing and jitter. Gear friction holds the arm in place.

CHECK before moving on Plug in the adapter and the UNO USB. Test sketch, type g: the servo moves CLOSED → OPEN three times and the UNO does not reset (the test menu does not reprint by itself). Unplug both.

11. Wire 5: the NodeMCU link

The NodeMCU sits on the front strip next to the breadboard and gets its own power from its micro-USB cable (laptop or a power bank). It is not plugged into the breadboard (it is too wide for a 400-point board); use male-female jumpers to its pins.

  1. Divider: put the 1 kΩ across two rows of the breadboard (row A and row B). Put the 2.2 kΩ from row B to the blue (GND) rail.
  2. UNO A3 → row A (the 1 kΩ's free end).
  3. Row B (the point between the two resistors) → NodeMCU D5.
  4. NodeMCU D6 → UNO A2 (direct wire, no resistor).
  5. NodeMCU GND → breadboard blue rail.
  6. Do not connect the NodeMCU's 3V3 or Vin to anything.
UNO A3 ──[1 kΩ]──●── NodeMCU D5
                   │
                [2.2 kΩ]
                   │
                  GND

Why the divider

The UNO speaks at 5 V; the ESP8266 chip on the NodeMCU is a 3.3 V chip. The divider turns 5 V into 5 × 2.2 / (1 + 2.2) ≈ 3.4 V, which the ESP8266 reads as HIGH safely. The other direction needs nothing: the UNO reads 3.3 V as HIGH.

ELI5 Two resistors share the 5 V like two friends sharing a pizza in proportion to their size: 1 slice for the 1 kΩ, 2.2 slices for the 2.2 kΩ. The NodeMCU only eats the 2.2 kΩ's share, about 3.4 V.

Why A2/A3 and not the UNO's TX/RX pins

The UNO's real serial pins (D0/D1) are used by the USB cable to the laptop (our backup path). So the sketch makes a second serial port in software on A2/A3 at 19200 baud.

CHECK before moving on Multimeter: with the UNO powered, measure between NodeMCU D5 and GND while the UNO is idle: about 3.3-3.4 V (serial lines rest HIGH). Never more than 3.6 V. The link test itself comes in Software 2.
GLOSSARY FOR THIS PAGE
Voltage divider: Two resistors in series that output a fixed fraction of the input voltage.
SoftwareSerial: A serial port made in software on ordinary pins, because the UNO has only one hardware port (used by USB).
Baud: Serial speed in bits per second (19200 between UNO and NodeMCU).
Common GND: All grounds wired together so every board measures voltage from the same zero.

13. Software 1: Arduino IDE, drivers and flashing

Install once

  1. Install Arduino IDE 2.x from arduino.cc.
  2. Plug in the UNO. If no new COM port appears (Tools > Port, or Device Manager > Ports), install the CH340 driver (the clone UNO uses this USB chip) and replug.
  3. File > Preferences > Additional boards manager URLs: https://arduino.esp8266.com/stable/package_esp8266com_index.json. Then Tools > Board > Boards Manager: install esp8266 by ESP8266 Community.
  4. Plug in the NodeMCU. If no COM port appears, install the Silicon Labs CP210x driver.
  5. Sketch > Include Library > Manage Libraries: install PubSubClient by Nick O'Leary. (Servo, SoftwareSerial and EEPROM are built in.)

Flash the hardware test sketch to the UNO

  1. Open firmware/uno_hardware_test/uno_hardware_test.ino.
  2. Tools > Board: Arduino Uno. Tools > Port: the UNO's COM port.

14. Calibration: sensors, gate angles, chute

A. IR sensors (trim pots)

  1. Test sketch, type s.
  2. Put a box in the lane exactly where it passes the sensor. Turn the trim pot slowly until the module's green LED just turns on, then a little further (about 1/8 turn).
  3. Take the box away: the LED must go off and the column read 0. If it stays on, the sensor is seeing the far rail: turn back a little, or put black tape on the front rail opposite the sensor.
  4. Slide a box through at normal speed 5 times: the column must flick to 1 every time.
  5. Repeat for all 6. Check again with the room lights you will have on demo day.

B. Gate angles

  1. Type g. Watch which way the shaft turns. The sketch uses 10° for closed and 95° for open.
  2. With the servo resting at the closed position (end of the test), push the horn on and glue the popsicle arm so it hangs down across the chute exit.
  3. Type g again. If the arm does not fully block or open, change GATE_CLOSED_DEG / GATE_OPEN_DEG at the top of both sketches (test and line controller), re-upload, repeat.
  4. Load 8 boxes behind the closed arm: none may slip past.

C. Chute + gate together

Type g with boxes loaded: each opening must release the front box onto S1. (In the real program the gate closes as soon as S1 IN sees the box, so only one box goes.)

17. Using the dashboard

  • Top tiles (scoreboard, idea from Vorne XL): good parts vs target, OEE with A/E/QR, throughput, WIP now / limit and gate state, bottleneck now, lead time with the Little's law check.
  • Line view: each station's lights copy the real LEDs; numbers between stations = boxes waiting.
  • CONWIP control: pick a limit (No limit, 2, 3, 4, 6) and press Apply limit. A new run starts automatically. "Mark last part scrap" logs a reject. "Reset line counts" only after all boxes are back in the chute.
  • Timeline: last 10 min of each station's active periods, orange = bottleneck, violet = shifting bottleneck, red = line down (hover for the reason), gate openings, WIP vs limit.
  • Bottleneck analysis (Roser), Downtime Pareto, Output per minute, KPI table with formulas (ISO 22400 names).
  • Run report (button top right): one row per run, for the A/B comparison; CSV download; print to PDF.
ELI5 The dashboard is a cricket scorecard: the big numbers are the score, the timeline is the ball-by-ball, and the run report compares innings.
GLOSSARY FOR THIS PAGE
OEE: Overall equipment effectiveness = availability x performance x quality.
Throughput: Finished boxes per minute.
Lead time: Time one box spends in the whole line, from S1 IN to S3 OUT.
Pareto chart: Bar chart of causes sorted from biggest to smallest, with a running total line.
Active period: A stretch of time when a station has a box on it (working or waiting to hand it on). Roser's method: the station with the longest current active period is the bottleneck.
ISO 22400: Standard that names manufacturing KPIs (availability, effectiveness, OEE...).

ISA-95: Standard that layers a factory into levels (machine, control, operations, business); our topic names follow its hierarchy.
Historian: A database that stores every machine event with its time (here: SQLite).
